## The `TileSet`: `resources/city_tileset.tres`

Generated by `build_resources.gd → build_tileset()`:

```gdscript
ts.tile_size = Vector2i(16, 16)
ts.add_physics_layer()                                  # physics layer 0
ts.set_physics_layer_collision_layer(0, 1)              #   → collision layer 1 "world"
ts.add_physics_layer()                                  # physics layer 1
ts.set_physics_layer_collision_layer(1, 1 << 4)         #   → collision layer 5 "platforms" (16)
```

A TileSet **physics layer** is a slot for collision shapes. Each slot is assigned a normal
collision layer. Two slots let ground and platforms sit on **different** collision layers, which
is what makes "drop through platforms but not the ground" possible.

Only the tiles listed in `GROUND_TILES` and `PLATFORM_TILES` are created. Every other cell of the
sheet is simply not a tile.

### Collision polygons

Tile polygons are in tile-local coordinates, **centred** on the tile (−8…+8):

```
Ground tile (full)            Platform tile (top 6 px, one-way)
(-8,-8)┌────────┐(8,-8)       (-8,-8)┌────────┐(8,-8)
       │████████│                    │████████│
       │████████│              (-8,-2)└────────┘(8,-2)
       │████████│                    
(-8,8) └────────┘(8,8)               
```

The platform's thin top strip plus `set_collision_polygon_one_way(1, 0, true)` means you only
collide when **landing from above**.

## Building the street (`build_level.gd`)

### Ground: `TileMapLayer` "Ground"

Three rows starting at `GROUND_ROW = 11` (y = 176):

| Row | Tiles | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | random of cols 1–4, row 22 | pavement surface |
| 12 | random of cols 6–9, rows 26–28 | curb / asphalt |
| 13 | random of cols 1–4, rows 26–28 | deeper street |

Randomly picking from 4 variants per row breaks up repetition. The layer is tinted
`Color(0.78, 0.76, 0.85)`, slightly darker and bluer than the buildings, so the floor reads as
"ground" rather than "wall".

Pits are just **missing cells**: `PITS = [[34, 37], [100, 103], [178, 181]]` (3 tiles = 48 px wide each).

### Platforms: `TileMapLayer` "Platforms"

Each entry `[first_tile, row, length]` draws a left cap, random middle pieces and a right cap.
Rows 9 (y = 144, 2 tiles above the ground) and 7 (y = 112, 4 tiles above) give two heights you can
reach with one jump.

### Buildings and props

Buildings are `Sprite2D`s with `region_enabled` cutting a facade out of `Tile1.png`. The helper
places a sprite by its **bottom-left corner**:

```gdscript
func _sprite(parent, region: Rect2, bottom_left: Vector2, tint := Color.WHITE) -> Sprite2D:
    ...
    s.centered = false
    s.position = bottom_left - Vector2(0, region.size.y)
```

The loop walks along the street choosing burger or yellow buildings (45 % / 55 %), darkens each a
random 0–12 %, adds signs (60 % on yellow buildings), 0–2 posters and 0–2 pieces of junk, then skips
50–130 px of gap. The RNG is seeded with **2026**, so the street is random but identical on every
rebuild.

## Depth: draw order and parallax

From back to front:

| Layer | Node | How it's ordered | Scroll |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sky gradient + stars + moon | `Sky` (CanvasLayer −20) | canvas layer | fixed to screen |
| Far skyline (dark silhouettes) | `FarCity` Parallax2D | z −10 | 0.2× camera |
| Mid skyline | `MidCity` Parallax2D | z −9 | 0.45× camera |
| Shop fronts, signs, posters | `Buildings` | z −5 | 1× |
| Street junk | `Props` | z −2 | 1× |
| Tiles, characters, projectiles | — | z 0 (tree order) | 1× |
| Rain | `Rain` (CanvasLayer 5) | canvas layer | fixed to screen |
| HUD | `HUD` (CanvasLayer 10) | canvas layer | fixed to screen |

* **`z_index`** orders nodes *within the same canvas*. Lower is drawn first, so it appears behind.
* **`CanvasLayer`** is a separate canvas with its own layer number, unaffected by the camera.
  Negative layers draw behind the main world (0), positive in front.
* **`Parallax2D`** (Godot 4.3+) moves its children by `scroll_scale` × camera movement. Values
  below 1 look far away. `repeat_size = (384, 0)` and `repeat_times = 3` tile the skyline
  horizontally forever.
* The skylines reuse the **same building regions** as the street, tinted almost black-purple
  (`Color(0.07, 0.06, 0.16)` and `(0.14, 0.12, 0.26)`). Tinting turns detailed art into
  silhouettes for free.

## Night atmosphere

| Effect | Node | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| Sky | `TextureRect` + `GradientTexture2D` | top `(0.04,0.03,0.10)` → bottom `(0.25,0.13,0.27)` |
| Stars & moon | `stars.gd` (custom `_draw`) | 70 one-pixel stars, sin-wave twinkle, crescent moon from 2 circles |
| Night tint | `CanvasModulate` "NightTint" | `Color(0.8, 0.77, 0.95)` multiplies everything on the main canvas (not CanvasLayers) |
| Rain | `CPUParticles2D` in a CanvasLayer | 140 drops, texture = 1×7 gradient (transparent → pale blue), direction (−0.25, 1), 260–320 px/s, `preprocess = 1` so it's already raining at frame 1 |

**Why the rain is on a CanvasLayer:** particles in world space would be left behind as the camera
moves. On a screen-fixed layer, the emitter always covers the view. Because drops fall fast, the
fact that they don't scroll with the world is invisible.
